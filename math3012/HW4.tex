    \begin{enumerate}
      \item In this question, we assume the houses are
        distinguishable. We group the 6 houses that have termites in
        one group. Then, we have 15 total groups to arrange, and 6
        houses to arrange within the `termite' group. This yields
        \begin{align*}
          N = 15! \cdot 6! \approx 9.4152554 \times 10^{14}.
        \end{align*}

      \item This is equivalent to arranging the 14 houses with no
        termites, and then choosing 6 out of the 15 possible
        positions to place the termite houses, and then arranging. This yields
        \begin{align*}
          N = 14! \cdot 6! \cdot {15 \choose 6} \approx 3.1415569 \times 10^{17}
        \end{align*}
    \end{enumerate}

  \item By subtracting the second equation from the first, the pair
    of these equations can be reduced into
    \begin{align*}
      x_4 + x_5 + x_6 + x_7 &= 6, \\
      x_1 + x_2 + x_3 &= 6.
    \end{align*}

    Therefore, we need to arrange 6 stars between 3 bars, and then
    arrange 6 stars between 2 bars. This can be done in
    \begin{align*}
      N = \dfrac{9!}{6! \cdot 3!} \cdot \dfrac{8!}{6! \cdot 2!} = 2352
    \end{align*}
    ways.

  \item Letting $x_1 = 1 + x',$ this will be the same number of
    integer solutions to the equation
    \begin{align*}
      x' + x_2 + x_3 &= 11, \text{ or} \\
      x_2 + x_3 &= 11 - x'
    \end{align*}
    subject to $0 \leq x' \leq 4,$ $x_2 \geq 0,$ and $x_3 \geq 0.$ We
    enumerate over all possible values of $x'.$ For each value of
    $x',$ we need to distribute $11 - x'$ stars over 1 bar. Therefore,
    \begin{align*}
      N &= \sum_{x' =0}^{4} \dfrac{(12 - x')!}{(11-x')!} \\
      &= \sum_{x' = 0}^{4} 12 - x' \\
      &= \sum_{k = 8}^{12}k = \dfrac{20}{2} \cdot 5 = 50
    \end{align*}
    ways.
\end{enumerate}
